## Closest non-competitor (the paper to cite and differentiate)

**Momennejad et al., "A rubric for human-like agents and NeuroAI", Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 378,
20210446 (2022)** - an Opinion piece proposing a qualitative 3-dimension rubric (human-like
behaviour / neural plausibility / engineering goals) for classifying what a study's goals are;
"the scale is meant to be qualitative". It NAMES the conflation problem ("success in one
dimension does not guarantee success in others" - isomorphic to our transition-chain premise)
but builds no measurement: no per-claim evidence grading, no corpus, no reporting layer, no
scorer. Positioning: the problem was named qualitatively in 2022; our instrument operationalizes
and measures it. This is the manuscript's strongest related-work anchor.

Also adjacent, not competitors:
- Chen et al., "How far is brain-inspired artificial intelligence away from us?", Front.
  Neurosci. 16:1096737 (2022) - evaluates the BRAIN-LIKENESS of AI models (a property of
  models), not the evidence papers supply for transfer claims.
- Hassabis et al. 2017 (Neuron) and the 2024-2026 survey wave (two inside our corpus) -
  inspiration mappings, class E: they organize directions, they do not grade evidence.
- "Brain-inspired AI needs new rules" coverage (Feb 2026, governance/assurance discussion) -
  calls for audit, provides no instrument (class C).

## Class B (adjacent ML meta-science instruments): EXIST - position precisely, do not overclaim

- Chong et al., "Measuring Reproducibility in LLM Research: A Rubric", ACM (2026) - a rubric
  assessing reproducibility of LLM papers, claims vs evidence presented. Paper-level.
- Walczak et al., "Three-pillar Evidence-based Methodological Framework for Experimental
  Machine Learning Studies", TechRxiv (2025) - scoring rubrics for ML experiments. Paper-level.
- ML Reproducibility Challenge methodology (Papers with Code) - explicitly "decouples the main
  claims in the reproduction from the evidence which supports those claims". Whole-claim level.
- SCORE (Systematizing Confidence in Open Research and Evidence) - crowd confidence ratings of
  research claims. Whole-claim level.
- Pineau et al., reproducibility checklists (NeurIPS family) - reporting completeness only;
  no evidence grading.
- Kapoor & Narayanan, Nature Machine Intelligence (2023) - leakage taxonomy; Longpre et al.,
  NMI (2024) - dataset licensing audit: NMI publishes this genre as regular Articles (genre
  precedent, already recorded in VENUE_DECISION_20261025.md).

Differentiator stated honestly: every class-B instrument is paper-level or whole-claim-level
and none targets the bio-to-AI transfer literature; the transition-level decomposition (a
paper can hold strong utility evidence and empty correspondence evidence - the difference IS
the finding) and the evidence-grade vs reporting-completeness separation are not present in
any instrument found.

## Class D (evidence grading outside AI): CONCEPT CREDIT REQUIRED

GRADE (clinical medicine, certainty-of-evidence per outcome) is the ancestor family and is
itself being AI-automated now (Dos Santos et al., BMJ EBM 2025/2026 proof-of-concept). The
manuscript must credit GRADE/EBM as the origin of evidence grading and claim only: the
transition-level decomposition, the separation from reporting completeness, the preregistered
kill rule, and the executable bound scorer, for THIS literature. Not a competitor (clinical
recommendation context, outcome-level, no transfer-chain structure).

## Honest novelty statement the manuscript may carry

Evidence grading as a concept is inherited (GRADE/EBM; SCORE); claims-vs-evidence auditing of
ML papers exists at paper level (Chong 2026; Walczak 2025; ML Reproducibility Challenge); the
NeuroAI conflation problem has been named qualitatively (Momennejad 2022). No published
instrument was found (2026-10-25, recorded queries) that decomposes bio-to-AI transfer claims
into their inference chain and grades, per transition, what evidence the claiming paper itself
supplies, separated from item-level reporting completeness, under a preregistered kill rule
with an executable scorer. That combination is the contribution claim; it is stated as
"no instrument was found on recorded queries", never as "none exists".
