# Separating neural structure, biological computation and artificial utility: an evidence-bounded case study

**NeuroConverge internal manuscript draft — 3 October 2026. Experiments are closed; this draft integrates the four Results, source-linked Methods, evidence-map interpretation and figure set. It is not submission-ready: source freeze, licensing, attribution, author information and venue-fit review remain open.**

## Abstract

Biological provenance does not itself establish artificial utility. We synthesize two heterogeneous evidence portfolios and report two separately analysed synthetic tests. The tested connectome assay and single-specimen Fish1.5 analysis did not establish a general structural advantage; its frozen topology-null inference was invalid. Published *C. elegans* evidence supports a bounded motor-state computation, while a planned animal-level reanalysis was schema-blocked. In a synthetic actuator-observability test, direct realized-state input did not improve action-conditioned AcRKN tracking (action-only minus realized-state MSE, −0.0681; 95% paired seed-block interval, −0.1411 to −0.0035). In a distinct synthetic timing task, a CF-inspired local rule benefited from preserved input–target correspondence, but same-feature generic ridge and exact replay showed larger effects. Neither test validates a biological mechanism or general transfer principle. These portfolios motivate separating source evidence, information correspondence and artificial utility; this retrospective framework remains unvalidated.

## Introduction

Biological systems are increasingly used to motivate artificial architectures, representations and learning rules. Yet three questions are often conflated: whether a biological structure is reliably characterized, whether it implements a particular computation, and whether an abstraction of that computation improves an artificial task. Evidence for one question does not settle the next.

NeuroMotif and NeuroMech approached different portions of this chain. NeuroMotif asked whether connectome-derived organization related to selected dynamical properties and whether a broader structure-to-AI claim could be supported. NeuroMech examined named biological computations, including motor-state feedback and cerebellar temporal learning, and tested simplified implementations under synthetic tasks. These histories are heterogeneous: they include source literature, source-data analyses, model reproductions, synthetic experiments, negative results, invalid analyses and blocked data paths.

Here we ask: **when does biologically grounded neural computation survive translation into a useful artificial inductive bias?** We treat this as a retrospective evidence audit, not as a pooled test or a claim that the projects were prospectively designed as one study. We organize the evidence around four questions: what the structure tests support; what source evidence identifies as computation; what artificial tests show under their actual controls and task inputs; and what remains a prospective transfer hypothesis.

## Results

We examined evidence at distinct levels: connectome-based structure tests, published biological computations and source-data analyses, and two synthetic experiments on state information and temporal correspondence. NeuroMotif and NeuroMech were independent research portfolios with different tasks and units of analysis; their outcomes were not pooled. The two V1 synthetic experiments were designed after earlier project outcomes, with contracts frozen locally before their outcome runs. They were outcome-informed extensions, not preregistered confirmations (Supplementary Methods S1 and Supplementary Table S3).

### 1. The tested connectome descriptions did not establish the proposed computational advantage

The whole-connectome assay did not establish an advantage of the tested off-diagonal topology for value retention. In the frozen reservoir/interference assay, connectome-based interactions did not outperform passive identity-like persistence in the interference range with recoverable signal. A crossover at stronger interference occurred only when both models approached noise-level retention. The comparator retained diagonal state persistence, so the contrast tested the added value of the specified interactions over an existing memory process. It did not compare recurrence with a memoryless system. This result bounded the tested topology, dynamics and task; it did not establish that connectome structure generally lacks computational value (Supplementary Table S1).

The separate Fish1.5 analysis asked whether a connectome-derived recurrence measure was positively associated with neuronal activity persistence. The predicted association was not supported among 82 neurons from one specimen: Spearman's ρ was −0.1096, the positive-direction permutation P value was 0.8337, and the neuron-bootstrap 95% confidence interval was [−0.2932, 0.1338]. This interval described variation under neuronal resampling within that specimen, not uncertainty across animals. Moreover, the frozen topology-null analysis could not provide valid inference: correlations were undefined for 861 of 1,000 null graphs, leaving 139 defined statistics. No valid topology-null P value was estimable under that procedure. The unsupported positive association and the invalid topology null were therefore retained as separate outcomes; neither justified a population-level structural conclusion. Together, these assays did not establish the proposed structure-to-dynamics advantage under their evaluated conditions.

### 2. Source evidence constrained the computation without selecting a unique artificial implementation

The motor-state case began with published evidence for a biological computation rather than with an artificial benchmark. Ji et al. reported RIM-dependent motor-state information in AIY and a role for corollary discharge in sustaining forward locomotion during positive thermotaxis in *Caenorhabditis elegans* ([Ji et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.68848)). This supported a bounded sensory–motor computation at the reported cell-class and behavioural scope. It did not identify the source signal as an exact measurement of displacement after actuator execution, the variable manipulated in the synthetic experiment below.

The project-level animal analysis could not independently resolve that biological account. The available source tables did not provide the animal/session identity and stimulus alignment required for the frozen reanalysis. Consequently, this path remained schema-blocked. Descriptive events could not be treated as independent animals, and the missing alignment prevented the planned test. The blocked result limited the project reanalysis; it supplied no evidence against the published biological computation (Supplementary Table S1).

The cerebellar case also separated biological observations from their modeled readout. Garcia-Garcia et al. studied granule-cell and climbing-fibre activity and an associated computation for tracking long intervals ([Garcia-Garcia et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.05.019)). In the project's exploratory held-out readout analysis, author-code-derived plasticity weights achieved higher held-out squared Pearson time-readout correlations than uniform weights in 15 of 16 source sessions and climbing-fibre-time-shuffled weights in all 16; a generic ridge readout outperformed the source-derived projection in 14 of 16. The ridge comparator was a descriptive readout reference, not a capacity-matched alternative mechanism. These were descriptive comparisons from within-session splits; squared correlation measured temporal association without preserving its direction or assessing calibrated timing, and sessions were not independent animal-level tests. The weights were model-derived, not measured synaptic strengths (Supplementary Results S2). Separately, optogenetic evidence for climbing-fibre instructive signals in mouse delay eyeblink conditioning supported that biological learning role ([Silva et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-024-01594-7)). It did not validate the particular artificial update equation used here. Thus, the source records supported specific computations while leaving the artificial signal, representation and learning rule to be assessed separately.

### 3. Direct execution-state information did not improve the tested action-conditioned controller

We next tested whether an additional state observation improved control in a synthetic actuator-observability problem. A one-dimensional tracking plant combined intermittent, noisy relative-position observations with stochastic actuator reversals. The action-only controller received its preceding command; the realized-state controller additionally received the exact sign of the preceding executed displacement. Both used the same adapted action-conditional recurrent Kalman network (AcRKN) cell architecture, with 320 trainable parameters and equal training budgets ([Shaj et al., 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/shaj21a.html)). Thirty-two paired training-seed blocks shared environment random streams, with 128 held-out episodes per arm and condition within each block. The independent inferential unit was the training-seed block, not the episode or time step (Supplementary Table S3).

Under hidden reversals, the realized-state observation did not improve the primary outcome. Mean squared tracking error (MSE) was 2.43809 for action-only and 2.50618 for realized-state. The frozen action-only-minus-realized-state contrast was −0.068094, with a paired seed-block bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [−0.141077, −0.003541]. Positive values would have favoured realized-state input; the observed interval instead favoured action-only under this implementation and training budget.

The diagnostic controls did not establish an information-specific explanation for this difference. Supplying the reversal-event bit alongside the command made the realized execution sign reconstructible. In that revealed condition, reversal-event-minus-realized-state MSE was +0.001992 (95% confidence interval, [−0.089678, 0.081652]). The interval included zero, but no equivalence margin was specified, so this did not establish equivalent performance. The corresponding information-recovery contrast also included zero (−0.070086; [−0.152817, 0.003712]). With no reversals, when execution sign was determined by the command, the action-only-minus-realized-state gap was −0.041911 ([−0.101355, 0.021932]). These diagnostics left input representation and training effects as unresolved explanations; they did not isolate a disadvantage caused by the additional information itself.

Generic recurrent and task-aware references achieved lower mean errors. Under hidden reversals, gated recurrent unit (GRU) controllers reached MSEs of 1.86456 with action-only input and 1.84687 with realized-state input, while the particle-filter reference reached 1.44413. Their mean errors were also lower than the AcRKN arms in the other two conditions (Supplementary Results S4). The GRU pair had 323 parameters each; the particle filter used known task dynamics and was not capacity matched. These comparisons situated AcRKN performance without isolating an architecture effect. Two complete executions reproduced the scientific outputs exactly after exclusion of declared timing fields. The result applied to one plant, an exact uncorrupted post-actuator sign measurement and a fixed training budget. That signal was distinct from the premotor/corollary-discharge signal in the worm source evidence.

### 4. Preserved temporal correspondence supported prediction without establishing mechanism specificity

The timing experiment tested a different question: whether a fixed local learning rule could exploit trial-specific temporal information when its correspondence with the target was preserved. Each synthetic input contained a noisy temporal pulse related to a target interval. For each of 30 independent task seeds, 1,200 training trials and 400 held-out trials were generated. The aligned regime paired inputs with their generating targets; the broken regime independently permuted target assignments within the training and test splits. The input arrays and target multisets were identical between regimes, so the manipulation changed correspondence while preserving those marginals. The climbing-fibre-inspired local rule used 16 temporal radial-basis eligibility features and a 28-class softmax readout. It was an artificial eligibility-gated update, not a literal model of measured cerebellar long-term depression (Supplementary Table S3).

The task met its frozen viability criterion. In the aligned regime, the full-input replay reference achieved normalized mean absolute error (nMAE) of 0.07138, compared with 0.25816 for the constant-median predictor, a 72.35% relative reduction; its paired correspondence-effect interval was above zero. For the local rule, aligned and broken mean nMAEs were 0.14152 and 0.25811. The primary broken-minus-aligned effect was +0.116588 (paired task-seed bootstrap 95% confidence interval, [+0.115072, +0.118065]), with a positive contrast in all 30 seeds. Normalization divided interval MAE by the 27-bin target range. Preserved correspondence therefore improved prediction by this fixed rule in the constructed task.

The temporal controls qualified that effect. Removing the temporal eligibility profile yielded a correspondence-effect estimate of −0.000070, while shuffling teaching-time assignments yielded −0.001199; both bootstrap intervals included zero (Supplementary Results S5). Generic learners exploited the temporal information more effectively: ridge regression on the same eligibility features achieved aligned nMAE of 0.05664 and a correspondence effect of +0.202390 (95% confidence interval, [+0.200123, +0.204675]). Full-input replay showed an effect of +0.189552 ([+0.186912, +0.192229]). The local rule thus demonstrated usable correspondence without outperforming these alternatives.

Comparator matching limited the specificity claim. The ridge arm shared the 16-feature encoder but fitted a 17-parameter scalar head; the local rule fitted a 476-parameter classification head. Their objectives and fitting procedures also differed. The frozen contract's parameter-matched description was corrected in the post-run audit to feature matched, preserving the original record. The comparison showed that a generic learner could use the same representation; it did not isolate a learning-rule effect at equal capacity. In addition, correspondence was changed in both training and evaluation, so its contrast did not separately identify those contributions. The supported result was a correspondence benefit for the tested synthetic implementations, with no established biological mechanism specificity or general transfer advantage.

## Discussion

The evaluated cases separated source support from artificial utility at several points. Structural descriptions did not establish their proposed dynamical advantage within the tested assays. Supported biological computations left artificial implementation choices unresolved. The synthetic experiments then showed different outcomes: direct execution-state access did not improve the tested AcRKN controller, whereas temporal correspondence helped a local rule and was exploited more effectively by generic alternatives. Their joint interpretation is that a source label, an informative representation and a useful implementation are distinct claims, each bounded by its own evidence.

We propose a neural transfer evidence map with four questions: source validity, computational interpretation, information correspondence and artificial utility. Source validity concerns the identity, coverage and limitations of the biological observations. Computational interpretation specifies the operation supported by recordings, models or interventions. Information correspondence asks which task-relevant variable or relation survives in the artificial input and representation. Artificial utility is a measured benefit under a stated objective and comparator set. Mechanism specificity is a separate question about whether the proposed operation explains an effect beyond alternative implementations. The map organizes the present cases; its categories are neither a causal sequence nor established necessary and sufficient conditions for successful transfer (Figs. 1 and 5).

The synthetic outcomes are compatible with several implementation-level explanations. In M2, additional information was available to a finite trained controller, so worse performance does not imply that the observation is intrinsically harmful or useless to an optimal estimator. The no-reversal and revealed-event diagnostics left representation and training effects unresolved. The GRU and model-based references also differed in inductive structure or access to task dynamics. In M5, ridge used the same eligibility features but a smaller scalar output head and a different objective and fitting procedure. Its advantage shows that those features support generic prediction, while leaving the learning-rule contribution unidentified at matched capacity. Because the primary correspondence manipulation changed both training and evaluation pairing, it cannot assign the effect exclusively to temporal credit assignment. Moreover, the features were computed from complete trial arrays, so the experiment did not demonstrate an online within-trial eligibility mechanism.

These findings qualify the evaluated implementations without overturning successful connectome-informed or biologically motivated models in other settings. Connectome reservoir models have shown task-specific utility, while recent work on connectome-constrained recurrent networks combines structural and functional evidence to predict activity (Suárez et al., 2024; Beiran & Litwin-Kumar, 2025). These precedents place the present negative or bounded results in context: they do not show that biological connectivity lacks computational value. Source circuitry can constrain a model without being the uniquely effective artificial realization of a computation. Conversely, generic prediction from a source-derived representation does not show that the source mechanism is absent or unnecessary in the animal. The evidence map makes those distinctions explicit, but these cases do not establish its novelty or generality across neural systems.

Several limits follow from the evidence units and selection history. Fish1.5 included one specimen and an invalid frozen topology null; neuronal resampling did not provide animal-population inference. The source readout used within-session splits and modeled weights. The two synthetic V1 tests were chosen after earlier project outcomes and used one generator and fixed training choices each, with no evaluated sensor corruption, broad task distribution or independent biological transfer. Historical invalid, blocked and unfavourable records remain in the supplementary portfolio to expose that selection history. Exact public linkage and redistribution rights are incomplete for some source artifacts. The current synthesis therefore supports only bounded implementation claims and identifies untested correspondence and specificity questions. Testing general transfer requirements would require independently selected tasks, source-aligned signals, matched alternatives and replication outside these portfolios.

## Methods

### Study design and evidence selection

NeuroConverge is a retrospective synthesis of two independently developed research portfolios, with two separately analysed synthetic extensions. It is not a systematic review, a prospectively unified biological study or a meta-analysis. We imported all 39 records from the NeuroMech Route-D V3 classification matrix and retained their original evidence labels, units, statuses and interpretation limits in Supplementary Table S1. Selected packages on the separate `experiment-publication` branch were crosswalked to that matrix (Supplementary Table S2). Some packages lack an exact matrix record; this limits the completeness of the source inventory, not the identities of the separately hashed packages. RR18 and RR19 belong to separately governed workstreams and were excluded from this synthesis.

Main-text selection followed the four questions stated in Results: structural evidence, source-supported computation, execution-state access and temporal correspondence. Selection was made with knowledge of earlier project outcomes. Historical positive, negative, invalid and inconclusive records were retained in the supplementary portfolio. An invalid analysis or failed viability gate was not interpreted as a valid negative mechanism test. Neither the two portfolios nor the M2 and M5 V1 endpoints were pooled. The V1 contracts were frozen locally before outcome execution, following inspection of earlier experiments; neither was preregistered or confirmatory.

### Whole-connectome reservoir assay

The locally archived M0 report evaluated neuron-level mappings of worm and larval fly connectivity under fixed reservoir dynamics and sustained interference. The recurrence implemented

\[
 h_t=(1-\lambda)h_{t-1}+\lambda\tanh(W_{hh}h_{t-1}+W_xx_t+b).
\]

The passive comparator set the off-diagonal recurrent matrix to zero while retaining the same leak and diagonal state persistence. Matched comparisons shared the input-weight draw, stimuli, interference realization, probe split and ridge regularization. Value retention was assessed using readouts of reservoir state under the predefined delays and interference settings. The manuscript uses the corrected report's bounded conclusion in the range with recoverable information, retaining its noise-floor crossover qualification. The underlying report also records node-exclusion, random-seed and probe corrections; earlier invalid implementations were not treated as independent results.

This manuscript did not rerun or reconstruct the complete M0 assay. The corrected report and available manifests are identified in the paper-critical source snapshot. Canonical round-level linkage is not fully reconciled, so the present Methods describe the supported comparator definition without assigning one unresolved configuration to every historical round. The report, scope of this limitation and source records are retained in Supplementary Results S3 and the reproducibility inventory.

### Fish1.5 structure and functional persistence

The Fish1.5 analysis used released structural annotations from the version-2 preprint source ([Boulanger-Weill et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.03.14.643363)) and functional calcium-response arrays from one specimen (Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.16893093). Of 97 structurally covered functional identities, the preselected primary cohort comprised 82 with strict structural completeness; the expanded 97-neuron graph was sensitivity-only. Graph construction used each mapped neuron's presynaptic owner table. Only annotations marked valid and partners mapped into the chosen cohort were retained. Self edges were excluded. Repeated synapse identifiers were collapsed only when partner coordinates agreed; ambiguous duplicates were rejected. Directed weights counted retained annotations rather than missing size fields. Postsynaptic files were integrity checks, not sources for filling absent primary edges.

For adjacency matrix W, where Wᵢⱼ is the directed weight from neuron i to neuron j, define out-strengthᵢ = ΣⱼWᵢⱼ and in-strengthᵢ = ΣⱼWⱼᵢ. The local recurrence index was

\[
 L_i=\frac{\sum_j W_{ij}W_{ji}}{(\mathrm{out\_strength}_i)(\mathrm{in\_strength}_i)},
\]

with zero assigned when the denominator was zero. The numerator is the diagonal of $W^2$, not of $WW^\mathsf{T}$. The functional endpoint used nominal timing defined by the released analysis code: 160 samples at 0.5-s spacing and dot stimulation during [20,60) s. Baseline, late-stimulus and post-offset windows were [0,20), [50,60) and [60,70) s. A trial required finite values in all three windows. Its persistence ratio was the baseline-subtracted mean post-offset response divided by the baseline-subtracted late-stimulus response. Exactly zero or nonfinite denominators were undefined and recorded, without an amplitude threshold or imputation. Direction-specific trial ratios were summarized by medians; the available left/right direction summaries were averaged per neuron.

The primary statistic was Spearman correlation between $L_i$ and persistence among eligible primary neurons. A positive-direction permutation test shuffled persistence labels 10,000 times and used the add-one upper-tail fraction. A 10,000-draw paired neuron bootstrap produced a percentile interval from defined correlations. The valid draw counts and the descriptive two-sided permutation result were retained in the source outputs; the bootstrap interval was conditional on the specimen and did not capture all network dependence or animal variation. Random streams were derived from seed 2026093001.

The frozen topology null generated 1,000 unique directed binary graphs by degree-preserving double-edge swaps, retaining the binary in/out degree sequences and global positive-weight multiset. Positive weights were randomly assigned to rewired edges; node-specific weighted strengths were not preserved. Every null correlation had to be defined for the frozen topology comparison to be valid. Undefined statistics were retained and the topology P value was left unestimated. Fixed secondary descriptors, Holm-adjusted secondary associations and robustness analyses remained distinct from the primary result. Phase lag and calibrated sine gain were not identifiable from the released schema.

### Biological source evidence and descriptive reanalyses

Published findings were attributed to their primary studies and were not represented as experiments performed for NeuroConverge. The worm source concerned RIM-dependent motor-state information in AIY and thermotaxis. The planned animal-level reanalysis required identities and stimulus alignment unavailable in the relevant released tables. A separate Figure 6C event-sheet summary retained event-weighted descriptions only; events were not used as animal replicates. These paths were kept distinct from the generic M2 synthetic plant.

The cerebellar source-readout analysis used the released Dryad v4 file `learning_1s_to_2s_GrC_CF.mat` and source-code-derived plasticity computation. All sessions in the three released groups were included: five 1-s expert sessions, five 2-s novice/1-s expert sessions and six 2-s expert sessions. Eligible rewarded trials satisfied the source `mvlen > 7` and `goodmvdir` criteria. Each session used five deterministic random 50/50 trial splits. Seeds were (20261001+100000g+1000s+k), with zero-based group, session and split indices. Climbing-fibre selection, activity scaling and readout weights were fitted exclusively on the training half.

Candidate climbing fibres showed a reward-aligned event increase in [0,0.25] s relative to [−0.3,−0.025] s. The source plasticity computation used granule-cell activity in [−0.15,−0.025] s before teaching events, with rounded frame offsets and inclusive window endpoints. Activity was rectified, scaled by training-cell 95th percentiles, transformed with the source logistic function, averaged and normalized per selected climbing fibre, then averaged with the source negative sign. These weights were model-derived proxies under the inherited connectivity assumption. Held-out readouts used movement-midpoint-aligned activity through the recorded reward delay, capped at 2 s, and squared Pearson correlation with elapsed time. Uniform, cell-shuffled and timing-shuffled controls and a training-standardized ridge reference with α=1.0 were evaluated on the same held-out trials. Valid trial scores were averaged within split, splits within session and sessions within source group. Two splits without training climbing-fibre candidates were retained as unusable. No animal-level inference was calculated, and the ridge reference was not capacity matched.

### M2 V1 plant and causal inputs

M2 V1 used a 64-step differentiable one-dimensional tracking plant. Target position $q_0$ was uniform on [−1,1], initial target velocity was Gaussian with SD 0.055, and subsequent velocity followed $v_t=0.91v_{t-1}+\epsilon_t$, with independent Gaussian innovations of SD 0.055; position was updated by velocity. Agent position began at zero and changed after a bounded command $a_t\in[-1,1]$ by $p_{t+1}=p_t+0.40a_ts_t$, where $s_t=-1$ indicated actuator reversal. Training reversal probabilities were sampled independently per episode uniformly from [0,0.25]. Held-out conditions used probability zero or 0.25; hidden and revealed reversals shared trajectories of environmental random variables.

Relative-position observations had Gaussian noise SD 0.14. Missingness used a two-state Markov process initialized with missing probability 0.5 and transition probabilities 0.125 into and out of the missing state, giving mean missing-burst duration eight steps. Six input features were supplied in fixed order: observed relative position or zero, visual-valid flag, previous execution auxiliary value, auxiliary-valid flag, previous command and visual gap clipped at 16 steps and divided by 16. The auxiliary value was zero with validity zero for action-only; the sign of previous executed displacement for realized-state; or +1 for a previous reversal and −1 otherwise for the revealed-event arm. Auxiliary validity began at zero. The revealed-event arm received its event channel during training and the no/revealed-reversal conditions, but not the hidden condition. None received a future or current unexecuted reversal event.

### M2 V1 models, training and reference

The adapted AcRKN cell used a six-to-three linear observation encoder, a six-dimensional latent mean and structured covariance, one transition basis, bandwidth two and a 26-unit action-control subnetwork. The policy head mapped the posterior mean through three tanh units to a tanh scalar command. Initial state and observation-variance parameters were learned. Mathematically inert one-basis coefficient-network parameters were frozen and excluded from the trainable count. Encoded observations were treated as valid at each cell update; modality missingness was represented by input flags. The observation update and policy preceded the action-conditioned prediction for the next step. The vendored cell retained its training-time dropout. This wrapper was an adaptation, not a complete reproduction of the original robotics model.

The three AcRKN arms each had 320 trainable parameters. The two generic GRU arms used the same six inputs, eight hidden units and a scalar policy readout, with 323 parameters each. Within family, initialization and environmental streams were paired. Each learned model used 160 Adam updates at learning rate 0.003, batch size 32, full 64-step sequences and global gradient clipping at five. The loss averaged squared target-relative distance measured before each step's new displacement. Gradients propagated through the differentiable rollout; the executed-displacement sign observation was detached. Each fit consumed 327,680 training time steps. Training and test streams were separately identified in the retained registry.

The task-aware particle filter used known target, observation and actuation distributions. Its 256-particle main evaluation inferred reversals when hidden and received preceding reversal events when revealed. Observation likelihoods updated weights, and systematic resampling occurred below half the particle count in effective sample size. The command was tanh(k_p e_hat − k_d v_agent_hat). Gains were selected from (k_p\in\{0.45,0.70,0.95,1.20\}) and (k_d\in\{0,0.15,0.30\}) on four disjoint validation seeds with 32 episodes each, using 64 particles and hidden reversals. The selected gains were held fixed in test evaluation. Particle counts 64, 128 and 256 and effective-sample-size diagnostics were retained. The reference was not matched in information, capacity or inference compute to learned models.

### M2 V1 endpoint and reproducibility

The independent unit was one paired training-seed block, with seeds 950000–950031. Each arm and condition used 128 test episodes per block. Episode-mean tracking MSE was averaged within block. The primary contrast was action-only minus realized-state AcRKN MSE under hidden reversals. Diagnostic contrasts comprised revealed-event minus realized-state in the revealed condition, the primary minus that revealed gap, and action-only minus realized-state under no reversals. Percentile intervals resampled paired block contrasts 20,000 times using seed 7813921. They were unadjusted exploratory intervals; no equivalence margin or portfolio-wide error control was specified.

Two full runs used Python 3.9.6, NumPy 2.0.2 and PyTorch 2.8.0 on CPU with one thread. Stored-output verifiers checked summaries, pairing, causal feature tests and hashes. Scientific files were compared byte for byte, excluding only four declared timing fields. Complete fit records, model parameters, episode metrics, stream identifiers, parameter counts and particle diagnostics were retained. Base source commit identity did not substitute for the captured contract, runner and verifier hashes in `SOURCE_PROVENANCE.json`.

### M5 V1 generator, features and learning

M5 V1 used task seeds 51000–51029. Each seed independently generated 1,200 training and 400 test trials with 40 bins ($t=0,\ldots,39$). Targets $y$ were uniform integers 6–33. Inputs were $x_t=\exp[-(t-c)^2/(2\times2.25^2)]+\epsilon_t$, with $c=y+\xi$, Gaussian $\xi$ SD 1.5 and independent Gaussian observation noise SD 0.20. Broken correspondence independently permuted training and test targets, preserving complete input arrays and target multisets. Permutations had no fixed trial indices; after at most 100 failed random draws, a cyclic shift was used. Repeated target values could still yield coincident labels after permutation. A separate permutation defined the shuffled-teaching arm.

The eligibility representation projected positive-clipped complete inputs onto 16 Gaussian bases with evenly spaced centres from 0 to 39 and width 2.75. Feature vectors were normalized by their Euclidean norm, with a (10^{-12}) floor, then augmented by a bias. This calculation consumed the full trial array; it was not an implemented online within-trial trace. The local rule processed generated training trials once in their existing order. With zero-initialized weights, class probabilities (r=\mathrm{softmax}(\tilde\phi^\mathsf{T}W)), and a one-hot target (z_y), the update was (W\leftarrow W+0.08\tilde\phi(z_y-r)^\mathsf{T}). Predictions were probability-weighted target-bin means. The teaching-time shuffle changed training labels for this update; evaluation retained each regime's actual targets. The no-trace arm used final-bin input and trial mean with bias under the same softmax rule, so it removed the temporal profile rather than every historical summary.

Generic ridge used the same 16 features and a scalar head. Full-input replay used all 40 input bins with a scalar head. Both fitted β by solving ((A^\mathsf{T}A+0.01D)\beta=A^\mathsf{T}y), where the bias penalty in diagonal (D) was zero, and clipped predictions to [6,33]. The generic ridge had 17 fitted output parameters; local/shuffled rules had 476, no-trace had 84 and full-input replay had 41. These arms differed in objective and fitting procedure as well as head capacity. The frozen contract's parameter-matched label was corrected after the run to feature matched without altering recorded outcomes.

### M5 V1 endpoint, gate and execution record

The sole performance metric was test MAE divided by 27 bins. The independent unit was task seed. The primary contrast was local-rule nMAE(broken) minus nMAE(aligned); comparator contrasts were descriptive secondary results. A deterministic 20,000-draw paired-seed percentile bootstrap used a generator initialized with seed 2026100105 and advanced across arms in the recorded order. These intervals were not multiplicity adjusted. Task viability required at least 25% relative mean improvement of aligned full-input replay over a training-target median predictor and a strictly positive lower interval bound for replay's correspondence effect. Conditional on viability, a positive primary lower bound supported the frozen correspondence claim; an interval spanning zero was classified as not detected, and an entirely negative interval as reversed.

The first launch failed before producing outcomes because Python 3.9 did not support two `zip(strict=True)` calls. A syntax-only amendment was frozen before the one successful run under Python 3.9.6 and NumPy 2.0.2. Original and amended freezes and execution-incident records were preserved. Reported NumPy/Accelerate warnings were audited against finite outputs and an independent contraction check; the independent result verifier passed. No arm, seed, model or generator was changed after outcome inspection. The manifest identifies the successful runner, contract and all scientific output hashes. The original labels in frozen files were preserved as historical provenance, even where the manuscript uses corrected terminology.

### Units, inference and writing assistance

Neurons within one specimen, source recording sessions, synthetic training-seed blocks and synthetic task seeds were kept distinct. Trial, episode, frame, null-graph and bootstrap counts were not described as biological replicate counts. No new animal experiment was conducted for this integration. Source-study ethical approvals remain those reported by the original authors; no approval identifier was invented or transferred to the present synthetic work. Published biological conclusions were not reclassified based on synthetic benchmark outcomes.

AI assistance was used to organize the evidence inventory, draft and revise manuscript prose, inspect code and source records, and generate internal-review figure layouts. Numerical reporting was reconciled with retained outputs and reviewed separately. This statement describes the assistance performed; final scientific interpretation, attribution and submission remain the responsibility of the human authors.
